  1. Minimal Surfaces in 3D; the case of two coaxial rings, and computing the different cases

    This was conducted as part of a first-year science Dalyell research unit at USyd conducting research under supervision. We managed to rederive the conditions on the parameters for which different minimal surfaces exist in the case of coaxial rings; I led most of the research effort with help from my supervisor, and derived most of the key results we got in the end. I also discovered a completely new result, but due to time constraints was unable to figure out why it was true.

    The new result, and a question!

    Consider the unstable catenoid with boundaries being congruent coaxial rings. The rings are separated with distance 2 d , with radii r . Consider the normal variation defined by f ( z , θ ) = d 2 − z 2 where the parameters are local coordinates in the obvious way. This variation does show that the unstable catenoid is unstable for large enough r / d , however this requires r d ≳ 1.509 as confirmed by using numerical integration techniques. This misses an interval of length ~0.0022, and is substantial enough to be of interest. The question is, why does this happen? Intuitively this particular variation should always reduce area when looking at the actual picture.
    Our original idea as to why was that our numerical integrator was having a bad time and some floating-point errors got through, completely ruining the calculation. However there may be some other, more abstract and interesting reason as to why this occurred. Currently the only way is to numerically compute a function which ends up being the integral of another function depending on the parameters.

Completed projects

  1. Drawing fractals using OpenGL and C++ (Github link)

    An old project as part of high school mathematical research on Schottky groups and fractals. In order to visualise the fractals generated to get a better understanding, I built a program in C++ that wrote an Asymptote (programming language used in concurrent with LaTeX occasionally to generate high-quality images) file that would compile into a PDF showing the fractal, original project link here. Later due to memory constraints on my old Macbook Pro (~8GB RAM which would blow by the time we reached 12 levels of recursion due to Asymptote's innate inefficiencies with memory), I switched Asymptote for OpenGL, along with floating-point for arbitrary-precision, to get a much faster and accurate renderer. Eventually managed to implement and include the cusp algorithm to plot the once-degenerate groups to get some stunning graphics.

    Languages used: C++, GLSL (later iteration)
